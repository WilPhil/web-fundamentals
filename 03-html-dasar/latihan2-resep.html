Tutorial on How to Make Classic Garlic Butter Pasta (Aglio e Olio style)

Viral in many countries. This pasta is certainly up to taste!

Cooking Terms Glossary:

Al dente
Refers to pasta cooked until tender on the outside but still firm to the bite in the center.
Emulsify
Combining two liquids that normally resist mixing into a smooth, unified sauce.
Sauté
Cooking food quickly in a small amount of oil or fat over direct heat.

Ingredients:

  • Pasta 200 gr

    • Spaghetti or

    • Linguine or

    • Fettuccine

  • Garlic 4-5 cloves

  • Olive oil 3 tbsp

  • Unsalted butter 2 tbsp

  • Chili flakes 1/2 tsp

  • Pasta water 1/2 cup

  • Salt & black pepper for taste

  • Fresh parsley or grated Parmesan for garnish

How To Make:

  1. Boil the pasta: Bring a large pot of heavily salted water to a rolling boil. Cook the spaghetti until al dente (usually 1–2 minutes less than package instructions).

  2. Save the water: Before draining, scoop out ½ cup of the starchy cooking water and set it aside. Drain the pasta.

  3. Sauté the garlic: In a large skillet over low-medium heat, add olive oil and the sliced garlic. Gently sizzle until the garlic turns light golden and fragrant (about 2 minutes). Do not let it burn!

  4. Add spice and butter: Stir in the chili flakes and butter until melted

  5. Emulsify the sauce: Pour ¼ cup of the reserved pasta water into the skillet. Swirl and simmer vigorously for 30–60 seconds until a glossy, cohesive sauce forms.

  6. Toss together: Add the drained pasta into the skillet. Toss continuously for 1 minute so the sauce coats every strand. (Add a splash more pasta water if it looks dry).

  7. Season and serve: Season with salt and pepper. Top with chopped parsley and grated Parmesan cheese.

Pro Tips:

  • Salt the water like the sea: Pasta absorbs salt while boiling; salting after it’s cooked won’t flavor the pasta noodles from the inside.

  • Low and slow for garlic: Garlic burns fast and turns bitter. Keep heat low—if it starts browning too fast, pull the pan off the burner for a moment.

  • Never discard pasta water: The starch dissolved in the boiling water acts as an emulsifier that binds the fat (oil/butter) and water into a creamy, restaurant-quality glaze instead of a greasy pool.
